Method

How every number here is worked out

A calculator that will not show its workings is asking to be trusted rather than checked. This page is the workings — including the parts of the model that are judgement calls, and the questions it cannot answer at all.

The cost model

Two adjustments, one baseline

Every figure starts as a US-market baseline in US dollars, current for 2026, assembled from published veterinary price surveys, pet-industry spending data, insurance claim averages and retail pricing for food and equipment. Nothing is a live feed; these are planning baselines that move slowly.

Those baselines are then adjusted twice before you see them:

Exchange rate

A straightforward conversion into your currency.

Local cost of living

A separate index applied to veterinary care, food and services. This matters more than the exchange rate: a consultation in Mumbai is not a New York consultation at the spot rate, and pretending otherwise would make the Indian figures roughly four times too high.

Both adjustments are visible below. If your city is materially more or less expensive than your country's average — and capital cities usually are — treat the output as a floor rather than a forecast.

Inside a year

What the annual figure contains

The recurring number is not just food. It is fourteen possible lines, of which the ones people forget are usually the largest:

Counted every year

  • Food, at your chosen quality tier
  • Bedding, litter, hay or substrate
  • Parasite prevention and routine medication
  • Toys, enrichment and consumables
  • Heating and electricity, for species that need it
  • Routine veterinary care and vaccination
  • Amortised dental work, where the species needs it
  • Insurance premiums, where a real market exists
  • Professional grooming at your chosen cadence
  • Walker or daycare days
  • Boarding or sitting nights
  • Pet rent, if your landlord charges it
  • Damage and replacement
  • Expected emergency cost

The emergency line

This is the one that makes people argue with the tool, so it is worth explaining. It is an expected value: the annual probability of a major incident multiplied by its typical cost. For a large dog that is roughly a 22% chance of a event, which shows up as about a year.

In any given year you will almost certainly not spend exactly that. You will spend nothing for four years and then a great deal in one night. Averaging it into the annual figure is the honest way to show a cost that is certain in aggregate and unpredictable in timing — and you can switch it off if you would rather see the optimistic version.

Deliberately excluded

Your time, which is the largest real cost and cannot be priced. Lost deposits, higher home insurance, the holidays you do not take, and the sofa you replace early. Treat every figure as a floor.

The readiness score

Six dimensions, and a hard ceiling

Twenty-one questions feed six weighted dimensions. Within each dimension, questions carry their own weight — whether you could absorb an emergency vet bill counts for considerably more than how tidy your daily routine is.

DimensionWeightWhat it is really testing
Time & attention24%Hours the home is empty, realistic daily time, routine, and whether that holds
Financial room22%Monthly slack, emergency capacity, insurance intent, income stability
Home & permission18%Tenure, written permission, space, household agreement, likelihood of moving
Life stability15%Travel, honest commitment horizon, large changes on the horizon
Support & skill11%A named backup carer, prior experience as the responsible adult, allergies
Motivation10%Why now, depth of research, and whether you have pictured the unglamorous version

Why some answers cap the total

Five answers do not merely subtract points, they put a ceiling on the score: a lease that forbids animals, a housemate who is not on board, no way to pay an emergency bill, a commitment horizon under three years, and a significant allergy in the household.

This is deliberate. A weighted average lets strength in one area quietly cancel a genuine blocker somewhere else, and a tool that told you a 91 was fine while your tenancy agreement said no would be worse than useless. Where a cap applies, the uncapped score is shown alongside it so you can see exactly what the blocker is costing you.

The matcher

Fifteen axes, then seven blockers

Each of the sixty-nine candidates carries a profile of twenty-one scored attributes — size, energy, daily time needed, grooming, shedding, allergen load, trainability, tolerance of solitude, noise, suitability with children and other animals, beginner-friendliness, space requirement, affection, heat and cold tolerance, cost tier, health burden and lifespan range.

Your answers are compared against those attributes on fifteen weighted axes to produce a fit percentage. Time alone and daily time carry the highest weights, because failing on either is what most reliably ends badly. A separate welfare axis penalises breeds carrying a heavy inherited health burden, which is why several extremely popular flat-faced breeds rank lower here than their temperament alone would suggest.

Separately, seven conditions act as hard blockers — a significant allergy against a high-allergen species, children under six against an animal poorly suited to them, ten-hour absences against an animal that cannot be left, and so on. A blocked candidate is not silently dropped. It is listed with the specific reason, because that list is where most people learn what is actually driving their result.

On breed profiles. Every attribute is a central tendency across a whole population. Breed is a real and well-evidenced predictor of behaviour, but it explains only part of it — individual variation within a breed is wide, and early handling and training matter enormously. Nothing here should override what you see in an actual animal.

The UAE map

Scraped at build time, never in your browser

The places map is generated by scripts/scrape-places.js and committed as a static file. The site has no back end, so nothing is scraped when you load the page — you are reading a snapshot, and the snapshot has a date on it.

The default provider is the Overpass API over OpenStreetMap. It is keyless, the data is openly licensed under the ODbL, and every record arrives with real coordinates rather than an address that needs guessing at. Each pin links back to the OSM object it came from, so anything wrong can be checked — and fixed — at source rather than in our copy of it.

The honest gap

OpenStreetMap covers UAE pet services well: at the last run it returned around 150 vets, pet shops, groomers and dog parks. It covers dog-friendly venues almost not at all — exactly six places in the entire country carried a dog=yes tag. A map claiming to show pet-friendly cafes on that basis would be a map of nothing.

So the scraper ships with a second provider for that category: a Firecrawl extraction pass over published listings, geocoded through Nominatim, storing the source URL on every record. It runs only when a FIRECRAWL_API_KEY is present. Venues found this way are marked as reported rather than verified, because a listicle is evidence that somebody said a place allows dogs, not evidence that it still does.

What we will not do

We will not fill the gap by writing a list of businesses from memory and presenting it as researched. Naming a real venue as pet-friendly without a source is a claim about somebody else's business that we cannot stand behind, and the person it inconveniences is the one who drives across town with a dog in the back. Every pin here traces to a source, or it does not ship.

Ratings

Why the stars are either licensed or absent

A rating is a public claim about a named business, so it comes from a licensed source or it does not appear. There is no scraped-rating path in this project, and that is a decision rather than an oversight.

We tried. Google Maps — where almost all UAE ratings actually live — serves a JavaScript shell whose HTML contains map tiles and no review data, and scraping it would breach their terms in any case. Directory pages looked more promising until we read the output: asked to extract a star rating, a language model will return 0 when it cannot find one. A test run produced 0.0 stars for a grooming salon with 38 reviews, and another gave a perfect 5.0 drawn from three. Published on a map next to somebody's business name, the first is defamatory and the second is noise.

So ratings come from the Google Places API or nowhere. The provider is written and waiting in scripts/scrape-places.js; set GOOGLE_PLACES_API_KEY and run npm run scrape:ratings to fill them in against the places already on the map. It never adds a place — it only answers "how is this one rated" — and each candidate passes the same name-similarity guard used for geocoding, so a rating cannot be attached to the wrong business.

Until that key exists, the map shows no stars and the rating filters are hidden rather than left dead. An empty control implying data we do not have would be its own small lie.

Honesty

What this cannot tell you

Whether you will love it

The thing that actually makes fifteen years of expense and inconvenience worth it is exactly the thing no model can score. A perfect fit on paper can still be a relationship that never quite clicks.

What your individual animal will be like

Temperament, health and habits vary hugely within any breed or species. A well-matched individual from a badly-matched breed beats the reverse every time.

Your local prices

Veterinary pricing varies by a factor of three within a single city. Ring two practices near you and ask for their consultation fee, neutering price and vaccination course. It takes ten minutes and beats any model.

Legal restrictions where you live

Breed-specific legislation, exotic-animal permits, strata and condominium rules and rental law all vary by jurisdiction and change often. Check locally before you commit to anything.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your browser

Every calculation on this site runs locally in JavaScript. There is no back end, no account, no analytics and no third-party script other than the web font. Nothing you answer is transmitted anywhere.

Two things are stored on your own device, in this browser's local storage: your chosen theme and region, and the summary of a completed readiness check so the matcher can offer to prefill from it. Clearing your browser data removes both, and nothing else depends on them.
